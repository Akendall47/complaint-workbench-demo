Clauses

  • Complaint handling
  • POL-1.1 Acknowledging a complaint
    Every complaint must be logged and acknowledged in writing on the day it is received, or on the next business day if it arrives out of hours. The acknowledgement names the case reference, the handler and the date by which the customer will hear from us. Staff must never ask a customer to repeat a complaint that has already been recorded. Complaints made by phone are summarised back to the customer before the call ends.
  • POL-1.2 Complaint clock and deadlines
    The clock starts on the day the complaint is first received through any channel, not the day it is logged. Payment complaints must be answered within fifteen business days. All other complaints must be answered within eight weeks. A handler who expects to miss a deadline must tell the team lead at least five business days before it falls, and must tell the customer why in plain words.
  • POL-1.3 Urgent and vulnerable customers
    A case is urgent when the deadline is close, when the customer describes a vulnerability or money hardship, or when fraud is still happening. Urgent cases move to the top of the queue and must be opened by a handler within one business day. The reason for urgency is written on the case so that the next person understands it without reading every note. Handlers must be patient and avoid jargon.
  • POL-1.4 Routing to the right team
    Each complaint is routed to the department that best matches the main problem the customer describes. If two departments could own the case, the handler chooses the one holding the more serious part and records why. A suggested route from the triage tool is advice only. The handler reads the notes and confirms or changes the route before any handover is made.
  • POL-1.5 Writing the final response
    The final response must be written in plain British English, in short sentences, without unexplained terms. It states what we found, what we will do and what the customer can do next if they are still unhappy. A second person checks any response that offers money above the handler's limit. Every response is saved on the case before it is sent.
  • Data and records
  • POL-2.1 Keeping accurate notes
    Case notes record what was said and done, by whom and when. Notes must be factual and free of opinions about the customer. A note is never edited after it is saved; corrections are added as a new note that refers to the old one. Dates, amounts and the channel used must be written exactly as the customer gave them so a reader can check them later.
  • POL-2.2 Personal data in summaries
    Names, addresses, account numbers, sort codes, phone numbers, email addresses and postcodes are removed before case text is sent to any automated tool. Staff must check that the masked text still makes sense. A summary written by a tool is a draft. The handler reads it against the notes and removes anything the notes do not support.
  • POL-2.3 Access to case records
    Staff may open only the cases they need for their work. Every view of a case, every change and every handover is written to the audit log with the person's identifier and the time. The audit log cannot be edited or deleted by complaints staff. Attempts to look at records without a work reason are reported to the team lead.
  • POL-2.4 Retention of complaint files
    A complaint file is kept for six years after the case is accepted and closed. After that period a file is reviewed and either archived or destroyed by the records team. Files subject to an open request, dispute or investigation are kept until it ends. Staff never keep private copies of complaint material on personal devices, email accounts or paper.
  • POL-2.5 Quoting sources in drafts
    Every claim in a draft summary must point to words in the case notes. If a statement is an inference rather than a fact stated in the notes, it must be labelled as an inference. A claim with no supporting quote is removed or rewritten before the draft is used. Handlers confirm each claim they keep, and the system records who confirmed it.
  • Fraud claims
  • POL-3.1 First response to a fraud claim
    When a customer reports payments they did not authorise, the handler first checks that the card or account is blocked and that the customer is safe from further loss. Only then are the details taken. The handler records the amount disputed, the date of transaction and the merchant name exactly as the customer states them, even if the customer is unsure.
  • POL-3.2 Card and police details
    For card payments the handler records the card last four digits and never the full number. If the customer has reported the matter to the police, the police reference is written on the case. If no report has been made, the handler writes that none exists and explains the customer's options without pressure. Missing details are chased within two business days.
  • POL-3.3 Handover to the fraud specialist
    A fraud case is ready for handover when the amount disputed, date of transaction, merchant name, card last four and police reference are all present on the case. If one is missing, the handler says so in the handover note and states what has been done to find it. The specialist can send a handover back if the missing detail is needed to decide the claim.
  • POL-3.4 Scams where the customer paid
    When the customer was tricked into making a payment themselves, the case is treated with care and without blame. The handler records how the customer was contacted, what they were told and which account received the money. The recipient bank is contacted on the same business day. The customer is told plainly that recovery is not guaranteed.
  • POL-3.5 Keeping the customer informed
    The customer receives an update at least every five business days while a fraud claim is open, even when there is no news. Updates say what has been done, what is waiting and who holds the case now. If the claim is refused, the response sets out the reason in plain words and explains how to take the matter further.
  • Financial difficulty
  • POL-4.1 Recognising money worries
    Handlers must listen for signs that a customer is struggling to meet payments, even when the complaint is about something else. If the customer mentions lost income, unpaid bills or fear of losing their home, the handler asks gently whether they would like help. The handler records whether a vulnerability was noted and what the customer said in their own words.
  • POL-4.2 Income and spending
    With the customer's agreement, the handler records monthly income and monthly outgoings, and the total arrears owed at the date of the call. Figures are written as the customer gives them. The handler does not judge the customer's spending or press for documents on the first call. Where the customer is distressed, the call is paused and offered again later.
  • POL-4.3 Pausing collections activity
    While a financial difficulty complaint is being reviewed, the handler asks the collections team to pause letters and calls for up to fourteen days. The pause is written on the case. It can be extended only by the specialist. Charges that arise during the pause are listed so the specialist can decide whether they should be refunded.
  • POL-4.4 Offering a payment plan
    The handler may explain the standard repayment plans and record whether a payment plan was offered and whether the customer accepted it. The handler must not promise a particular outcome. Any arrangement that reduces or freezes interest or charges is agreed by the specialist. The customer receives the agreed terms in writing before the first payment is due.
  • POL-4.5 Handover to the financial support specialist
    A financial difficulty handover must show monthly income, monthly outgoings, total arrears, vulnerability noted and payment plan offered. If any of these is missing, the handler states why and what was tried. The specialist reads the notes and may send the case back with a clear request. A customer must never be asked to explain their circumstances twice.
  • Bereavement
  • POL-5.1 First contact after a death
    The first call about a death is handled by one named person wherever possible. The handler offers condolences, says what will happen next and avoids asking more than is needed. The handler records the date of death and the caller's relationship to deceased. No fees or interest are applied to the account while the notification is being processed.
  • POL-5.2 Dealing with the executor
    Only the executor or a person with legal authority may give instructions about the accounts. The handler records the executor name and the document that proves the role. If the caller is not the executor, the handler explains what can be shared and what cannot, and offers to speak to the executor directly. The explanation is kind, short and clear.
  • POL-5.3 Accounts and proof of death
    The handler lists every product held by the deceased as accounts affected, including joint accounts, cards, loans and savings. A death certificate must be seen before accounts are closed or money is released, and the handler records whether the death certificate seen is the original or a copy. The document is returned to the sender safely and the return is noted on the case.
  • POL-5.4 Complaints about how a death was handled
    If a family member complains about the way a bereavement was handled, the case is marked urgent. A senior handler reads all previous notes before calling. The call begins with an apology where one is due. The handler explains what went wrong, what will be corrected and by when. The family is given one direct contact number for the case.
  • POL-5.5 Handover for bereavement cases
    A bereavement handover must include the date of death, executor name, relationship to deceased, accounts affected and whether the death certificate was seen. Where a field is unknown, the handler writes unknown and records how it will be found. The receiving team must not ask the family for details that are already on the case.
  • Data protection
  • POL-6.1 Recognising a data request
    A customer asking what information we hold about them is making a data request, even if they do not use those words. The handler logs the data requested in the customer's own terms and records the request date as the day it was first made. The one month response period runs from that date, and the case is passed to the data protection team the same day.
  • POL-6.2 Checking identity
    Before any personal data is released, the handler confirms that the person asking is the customer or is acting with written permission. The handler records identity verified, with the evidence seen, and never copies identity documents onto the case. If identity cannot be confirmed, the handler explains what is needed and the clock is paused only when the specialist agrees.
  • POL-6.3 Searching our systems
    The handler lists the systems searched, including the case system, call recordings, card records and any archive. The data protection specialist decides whether further systems must be searched. Records about other people are marked for removal before release. The search is repeated if the customer later names a record that was not found the first time.
  • POL-6.4 Data shared in error
    If a customer says their details were sent to the wrong person or address, the handler treats the case as urgent and informs the data protection specialist at once. The handler records what was shared, who received it and how we found out. The customer is told what has been done to get the information back and what they can do to protect themselves.
  • POL-6.5 Handover to data protection
    A data protection handover must state the data requested, request date, whether identity was verified and the systems searched. The specialist can return a case that omits any of these. The handover also notes whether the customer has asked for a correction or deletion, because these are handled separately from a request for a copy.
  • Mis-selling review
  • POL-7.1 What counts as a sale complaint
    A complaint that a product or advice was unsuitable, misdescribed or sold without proper explanation belongs in the mis-selling review team. The handler records the product name and the date of sale as the customer remembers them, then checks them against the account records. If the two disagree, both are written down and the difference is noted for the reviewer.
  • POL-7.2 Sale channel and adviser
    The handler records the sale channel, such as branch, phone or online, and the adviser name if the customer can give it. If the customer does not know the adviser, the handler searches the sales records and notes the search. The handler never suggests that the adviser acted wrongly. The review team decides that after reading the sale file.
  • POL-7.3 Describing the loss
    The handler writes down the amount lost as the customer calculates it and says how the figure was reached. The handler explains that the review team will work out any loss itself, and records whether the loss explained to the customer was understood. Charges, missed returns and fall in value are listed separately so the reviewer can tell them apart.
  • POL-7.4 Gathering the sale file
    Within three business days of receiving a sale complaint, the handler requests the sale file from the archive. This includes the fact find, the risk questionnaire and the suitability report. The handler checks that the file is complete and lists any missing document. A missing document is reported to the team lead and does not delay the acknowledgement to the customer.
  • POL-7.5 Handover to the review team
    A mis-selling handover includes the product name, date of sale, sale channel, adviser name, amount lost and loss explained. The handler adds a short neutral summary of what the customer says happened. The review team may return a handover that lacks any of these items. Returned cases come back to the original handler with a written reason.
  • General service
  • POL-8.1 Understanding the problem
    For service complaints the handler writes an issue summary in one or two plain sentences, then checks it with the customer. The handler records the date of incident and, if the problem has happened more than once, lists the dates separately. The handler thanks the customer for raising the matter and says who will look into it.
  • POL-8.2 Branch or channel
    The handler records the branch or channel where the problem occurred, whether a branch, the phone line, the mobile app or the website. If the issue involves a named member of staff, the name is noted without comment. The handler checks whether other customers reported the same problem recently and tells the team lead if a pattern is appearing.
  • POL-8.3 What the customer wants
    Every service complaint records the customer outcome wanted, for example an apology, a refund or a change to a process. The handler asks this near the start of the case and records the answer in the customer's words. If the outcome cannot be given, the final response explains why and offers the nearest alternative the bank can provide.
  • POL-8.4 Digital service faults
    For faults with the website or app, the handler records the device, the version of the app and the time of the problem. The handler checks the service status log for known outages on that date. If several customers report the same fault within a week, the handler tells the digital team and the team lead and adds a note to each affected case.
  • POL-8.5 Goodwill payments
    A handler may offer a small goodwill payment up to their personal limit when we have made a mistake and the customer has lost time or money. The reason and amount are written on the case. Any larger amount needs the approval of the team lead before it is offered. A goodwill payment never replaces a refund of money the customer is owed.
